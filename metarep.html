Replication Probability from Meta-Analytic Evidence

Answers the question: "Given this meta-analysis, what is the probability that the next study will find a significant result in the same direction?"

The Formula

Let theta be the pooled effect (log scale), SE its standard error, tau^2 the between-study variance, and se_new the SE of a hypothetical new study. The true effect in the next study is theta_new ~ N(theta, tau^2), and the observed result is Y_new ~ N(theta_new, se_new^2). Marginally:

Y_new ~ N(theta, tau^2 + se_new^2)

The replication probability (significance + correct direction) is:

P(rep) = Phi((|theta| - z_alpha * se_new) / sqrt(tau^2 + se_new^2))

where Phi is the standard normal CDF and z_alpha = z_{alpha/2} for a two-sided test.

Key Insight

Classical power analysis assumes the true effect equals the pooled estimate. MetaRep incorporates heterogeneity (tau^2) into the prediction, recognizing that the next study's true effect will differ from the pooled average. This is why "predictive power" is always ≤ classical power when tau^2 > 0.

Expected Replication Rate

For each of the k studies already in the meta-analysis, we compute the probability that a study of the same size would replicate, then average. This tells you: "how many of the existing studies would you expect to replicate if you re-ran them?"

Limitations

  • Assumes normal distribution for effects (may not hold for small studies or rare events)
  • Uses the pooled estimate and tau^2 as known — does not account for estimation uncertainty
  • Replication is defined as p < alpha AND same direction — not exact replication of effect size
  • Does not account for publication bias or p-hacking in the original studies
